Self-Replay Forcing — Rolling Out on Your Own Frames

Paper: Vidu S2: Real-Time Interactive, Editable, and Spatial Video Generation — Jintao Zhang et al. (Tsinghua University · Shengshu Technology), arXiv:2609.11638 · demo. Defined against Self Forcing (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2025); inference stack from TurboDiffusion and TurboServe.

✅ Conclusion

  • The charge against Self Forcing: its self-generated history comes back clean and detached. Both are literally in its Algorithm 1 — line 13 caches at timestep 0, and the paper says it restricts gradient flow into the KV embeddings.
  • A clean cache costs a fifth forward pass per chunk. None of the 4 denoising passes ever had clean input — the clean x̂0 is an output. At 25–42 FPS Vidu will not pay that 25% tax, so it keeps pass 4's KV and lives with tau > 0.
  • Detaching deletes one specific term — the one saying "the problem started earlier". In a toy three-chunk chain the gradient drops 8.25 → 5.25, and the missing 36% is entirely cross-chunk.
  • SRF = roll out with the graph off, replay a short window with it on. Memory scales with the window you pick, not with stream length. Roll out 100 chunks, replay 6.
  • The catch: no SRF ablation and no latency table, in a paper whose whole claim is real-time. The human-preference percentages are all multiples of 1/7 — that is 7 comparisons per cell.

📄 Paper Summary

Vidu S2 is a real-time streaming video system in two halves — S2-Avatar, an interactive digital character at 720p and 25–42 FPS whose reference image can be swapped mid-stream, and S2-Editing, which edits an incoming video stream on the fly for style, clothing, subject and background via frame-aligned attention, where each target frame reads only the source frame at the same time step. Its training contribution is Self-Replay Forcing: the student rolls out on its own output with the computation graph switched off, a short window of that trajectory is re-noised, and only that window is replayed in one gradient-enabled causal pass — so the history is self-generated and noisy and connected, closing the two places Self Forcing's training history diverges from what the model meets at inference. Speed comes from two earlier systems by the same group, which Vidu S2 cites as [6] and [7] and adopts selectively:

TurboDiffusion [6]
A model-level accelerator for video diffusion, reporting 100–200× end-to-end speedup: step distillation (rCM) cuts ~50 denoising steps to a handful, on top of low-bit attention (SageAttention), Sparse-Linear Attention, and W8A8 quantization on INT8 tensor cores. Vidu S2 takes the kernels but applies them layer-wise — aggressive approximation on tolerant layers, accurate attention on sensitive ones — plus per-block W8A8 GEMM, kernel fusion and CUDA Graphs.
TurboServe [7]
The serving-level counterpart. Streaming sessions are long-lived and stateful — the KV cache must survive idle periods — so it frames serving as online scheduling: migration-aware placement across GPUs plus load-driven autoscaling, reporting 37.5% lower worst-case per-chunk latency and 37.2% lower GPU cost. Vidu S2 borrows the scheduling idea at a smaller scale, sharing one GPU timeline across the VAE encoder, backbone, Refiner and decoder instead of pinning GPUs per module.

In short: TurboDiffusion makes one stream fast on one GPU, TurboServe decides which GPU each session lives on, and Vidu S2 is the model that runs on top of both.

1. What a computation graph is, and what detach costs

When you write y = theta * 3, the framework does not only store y. It also writes a note: "y came from multiplying theta by 3." Stack a million notes and you get the graph. Backprop is walking it backwards to ask "if I nudge theta, how much does the loss move?"

Two facts that pull against each other: the graph costs memory, because every intermediate value must stay alive; and no graph means no learning through it. detach() is the scissors — keep the number, drop the notes. So "no gradient" and "no graph" are the same event seen from two sides. Cutting the graph is how you stop the gradient.

Three chunks, each taking its own noise input plus the previous chunk's output, one weight theta = 0.5, noise input 2 each time:

y1 = 0.5 * 2       = 1.0
y2 = 0.5 * (2+1.0) = 1.5
y3 = 0.5 * (2+1.5) = 1.75

target = 1.0   loss = (1.75-1.0)^2 = 0.5625   dL/dy3 = 1.5

Gradient on theta

8.25
  • chunk 3 itself  1.5 x 3.5 = 5.25
  • through chunk 2  1.5 x 1.5 = 2.25
  • through chunk 1  1.5 x 0.5 = 0.75

What the model learns

“Chunk 3 came out too high — and chunks 1 and 2 set it up badly. Fix all three.”

Full gradient = 8.25. Every chunk that contributed to the mistake gets corrected.

The point is not that 5.25 < 8.25. Detaching deletes one specific piece of information — "the problem started earlier". The model can only ever learn "fix the last chunk", never "stop setting yourself up to fail". Over a 5-minute stream, that is the missing piece you wanted.

Worth separating the two truncations in Self Forcing's Algorithm 1, because only one of them is at issue. The stochastic one — keep gradient at a single randomly drawn denoising step s per chunk — is a pure memory measure and SRF keeps the same spirit. The second one is the KV detach, stated outright: "we additionally detach the gradients of the previous frames from the current frame during training by restricting gradient flow into KV cache embeddings." That is the one SRF reworks. Its effect is that the backward pass sees seven islands with no arrows between them.

2. The output is clean. The cache is a separate object.

This is where I went wrong first. The video handed to the user is always fully denoised — never in question. But the KV cache is not the video; it is the internal attention memory, and the two can sit at different noise levels. Count the forward passes for one chunk with a 4-step denoiser:

pass 1:  x_t4 (pure noise) --model--> x̂_0 --renoise--> x_t3
pass 2:  x_t3             --model--> x̂_0 --renoise--> x_t2
pass 3:  x_t2             --model--> x̂_0 --renoise--> x_t1
pass 4:  x_t1             --model--> x̂_0   <-- clean, shown to user

Not one of those four passes had clean input. Pass 4's input was x_t1, still slightly noisy; the clean x̂_0 is its output. So a clean cache needs a fifth pass — feed x̂_0 back in at timestep 0 purely to harvest its keys and values. Self Forcing does exactly that, in a call separate from the denoising call (Algorithm 1 line 13, Algorithm 2 line 9):

line 6:   x̂_0  <- G(x_tj ; tj, KV)      # denoising loop
line 9:   kv_i <- G_KV(x̂_0 ; 0, KV)     # separate call, timestep 0

A 25% tax on a 4-step model. Vidu runs at 25–42 FPS and declines to pay it: keep pass 4's KV, live with tau > 0. Only one entry per chunk is stored, not four — the four passes are successive refinements of the same content, and keeping all of them would quadruple both cache memory and attention cost, and attention is already the stated bottleneck.

Because the kept KV comes from the lowest nonzero noise level, the cache is "almost clean". That makes the leak subtle rather than dramatic — but it is systematic and always in the same direction, and a 5-minute stream is hundreds of chunks. Small consistent bias plus compounding is how drift happens.

The usual case against noisy caches does not apply here — it is not the same "noisy". The standard objection targets a cache whose entries are provisional: a frame sits at noise 500 and keeps getting cleaner as the window moves. Vidu's chunk is final — fully denoised, shown, never revisited; only the cached K/V happen to come from a pass whose input was slightly noisy. So:
  1. Entry must be final — it is. Never recomputed, so no O(L²) rebuild.
  2. Cache grows a timestep dimension — it does not. One entry per chunk, at one tau.
  3. Latency — fine. The clean x̂0 is displayed immediately.
  4. Temporal consistency — this one lands. Blurred detail in the cache cannot be copied forward, so fine texture drifts.
And objection 4 is what Vidu's TwinCache answers: the backbone reads a high-noise cache tau_B to carry coarse motion undisturbed by high-frequency artifacts, while the Refiner reads a low-noise high-resolution cache tau_R that, in the paper's words, "preserves local appearance and identity cues". Motion and detail get separate caches. A clean cache would force tau_B = 0 and collapse that split — so for Vidu the noisy cache is load-bearing architecture, not only a shortcut. (Mapping the objection onto TwinCache is mine; the paper does not draw the line.)

Four recipes, one question: what do you show the model as history during training?

My first worry was that a graph over all history must be ruinous. It would be — a rollout is M x T forward passes, and at M=100, T=4 that is 400 passes of activations. SRF's answer is that the graph covers a window, not the stream. The paper samples a segment to replay "while the external history inherited from the detached rollout remains fixed." Three zones:

So memory is a dial you turn, decoupled from stream length. The loss is L_SRF = L_DMD(...) + L_perc(...), where the perceptual term (carried from Vidu S1) exists to stop mode collapse — DMD alone will happily settle on the same safe average face every time, because that scores well and risks nothing. "On-policy" just means the trajectory came from the current student under its real inference procedure.

The honest extra cost: SRF's backward now runs through cross-chunk attention inside the window, which the disconnected version skips entirely. That is real compute, roughly proportional to W, and the paper gives no number for it.

Once you see the pattern — detached rollout, gradient-enabled replay — it appears three times: SRF itself with a DMD loss, Streaming NFT with a reward loss, and S2-Editing, which the paper says "follows the same hybrid forcing and Self-Replay Forcing framework".

4. What I'd want before believing it

  1. No SRF ablation. This is the headline contribution and there is no table isolating it against plain Self Forcing. Both leaks are real and verifiable in Algorithm 1 — but "real problem" and "fixing it measurably helps" are different claims, and only the first is established.
  2. Leak 1 is partly Vidu's own doing. Self Forcing is internally consistent: it trains clean and runs clean, having budgeted for that fifth pass deliberately. It only becomes a mismatch because Vidu chose a noisy cache for speed and for the Refiner split.
  3. Leak 2 was a known trade-off, not an oversight — Self Forcing says outright that it detaches to bound memory. SRF found a wiring that avoids the price, which is good engineering on a stated trade-off, not a discovered bug.
  4. W is never specified, nor how tau is sampled per chunk, nor whether it matches the inference cache level, nor the M:W ratio. You could not reimplement this from the paper. My three-zone diagram is read between the lines of one sentence, not stated.
  5. No latency table anywhere, in a paper whose whole claim is real-time. "25~42 FPS" appears once, in the intro, GPU unnamed — and the paper itself writes that "product-page claims about frame rate or latency do not replace measured results".
  6. The human study is tiny. Every percentage in the avatar GSB figure is a multiple of 1/7, so "100% preferred" means 7 out of 7.
  7. Spatial video is a demo, not a result — no benchmark, no metric, no baseline, and the paper says so itself.
