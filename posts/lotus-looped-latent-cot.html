LOTUS — Supervising Looped Latents with the Gold CoT

Paper: Bridging the Gap Between Latent and Explicit Reasoning with Looped Transformers — Ying Fan, Anej Svete & Kangwook Lee, arXiv:2606.31779. Code: yingfan-bot/lotus.

✅ In one line

Keep a looped transformer, give it 6 blocks of blank "latent" slots, loop 6 times, and put a small cross-entropy loss on each slot against its gold CoT token. At test time the CoT is never written out — the answer reads the hidden vectors directly. On Llama-3.2-3B this reaches 70.0% on GSM8K vs 71.5% for explicit CoT, with a 2.5× faster thinking phase.

Step 1 — The gold CoT (from an existing dataset)

The authors do not build the CoT themselves. They use GSM8K-Aug (Deng et al. 2023). Original GSM8K solutions contain calculator tags inside sentences; GSM8K-Aug drops the sentences, keeps only the tags, and grows ~7.5K training problems to 385,620 (the growing was done by Deng et al., with GPT-4).

GSM8K:      Janet sells 16 - 3 - 4 = <<16-3-4=9>>9 duck eggs a day.
            She makes 9 * 2 = $<<9*2=18>>18 every day at the market.  #### 18

GSM8K-Aug:  <<16-3-4=9>>  <<9*2=18>>  #### 18
            \__ step 1 _/  \_ step 2 _/

One <<…>> = one step. 99% of examples have ≤ 6 steps, hence K = 6. A natural-language version (Wu et al. 2025, each step a full sentence) is used as a stress test.

Step 2 — Build the input: blank slots between question and answer

Q  <BoT>  [lat × 25]  [lat × 25]  [lat × 25]  …  [lat × 25]  <EoT>  A
           \ block 1 /  \ block 2 /                \ block 6 /
           target:       target:                   (no step 6 → padding, no loss)
           <<16-3-4=9>>  <<9*2=18>>

Each gold step is tokenized and padded or cut to 25 tokens; padding gets no loss. Steps beyond the 6th get no step loss and fall back to autoregressive completion.

Step 3 — Loop: refine all 150 slots together, 6 times

The question is encoded once into a KV cache. Then the same LM runs over the latent slots R = 6 times. Each loop gets the blank embeddings plus the previous loop's hidden vectors (input injection): h(0) = f(E | Q),   h(t) = f(E + h(t−1) | Q). Gradients flow back through all 6 loops. All 150 slots are processed in parallel in each loop — 6 sequential passes in total, not 150.

Step 4 — Step loss: read each slot through the LM head

After the last loop, every slot goes through the normal LM head and gets a CE against its own gold token:

block 1:  slot 1 → "<<"   slot 2 → "16"   slot 3 → "-"   slot 4 → "3"  …  slot 8 → "9"
block 2:  slot 1 → "<<"   slot 2 → "9"    slot 3 → "*"   …

All slots are scored at once. No slot sees the earlier gold tokens.

Step 5 — Answer loss: a second pass that reads the hidden vectors

In a separate pass, the post-loop hidden vectors h(6) are inserted at the latent positions, and the answer "#### 18" is trained with ordinary next-token CE. The latents are never turned into tokens for this — the answer reads the vectors. Total loss = Lans + 0.05 · Lstep.

Step 6 — The aux variant (LOTUS-aux)

Same loop, same answer loss; only the step loss is routed differently. An auxiliary decoder (a full copy of the base LM, trained together with it, used only in training) reads block t at loop t as a 25-vector prefix and predicts the gold step token by token with teacher forcing:

aux decoder input:   [25 latents of block 1]  <<   16   -    3   …
predict:                                  <<   16   -    3    -   …

Step 7 — Training recipe

Start from an explicit-CoT checkpoint on the same data. Then a Coconut-style curriculum: every epoch, one more written step becomes a latent block, and the loop count grows with it (R = number of blocks):

epoch 0:   Q  <<16-3-4=9>>  <<9*2=18>>  18     ← plain CoT
epoch 1:   Q  [block 1]     <<9*2=18>>  18     (R = 1)
epoch 2:   Q  [block 1]     [block 2]   18     (R = 2)
…
epoch 6+:  Q  [6 blocks]                18     (R = 6, final)

30 epochs, batch 128, AdamW, lr 5e-5 on 3B.

🖊 My Key Notes

The question I kept asking: how is the thinking supervised?

Every method below uses plain cross-entropy. What differs is which positions get a label and what the model sees as input while learning.

MethodLoss on the thinking?How the thinking learnsWrites CoT at test time?
Explicit CoTyes — next-token CE on every reasoning tokendirectly from labelsyes
Looped TF (paper setting)no — answer CE onlygradient back through the loops (up through depth)no
Ouroyes, if the text has reasoning — next-token CE on every token, each token looped 4×labels + extra depth per tokenyes (Thinking version)
Coconutno — the ● vectors get no loss; curriculum starts from CoTgradient back through the ● chain (sideways along positions)no
LOTUSyes — CE on each latent slot vs its gold token, weight 0.05labels on the latents + loops + answer gradientno

So LOTUS fixes exactly the gap of the pure looped TF and Coconut: their thinking had no label at all.

LOTUS step CE vs normal per-token CE

Both put a CE on the gold CoT tokens. The difference is the input and what happens at test time. Gold step 1 = <<16-3-4=9>>.

Explicit CoT / Ouro (per-token CE, teacher forcing):

input:    Q  <<   16   -    3    -    4    =    9   >>
predict:     16   -    3    -    4    =    9    >>  …

Each position sees the true earlier tokens and predicts the next one. To predict "9", the input already contains <<16-3-4=.

LOTUS (CE on latent slots):

input:    Q  [lat] [lat] [lat] [lat] [lat] [lat] [lat] [lat] [lat] …   (blank)
          → 6 loops over all slots together →
predict:     <<    16    -     3     -     4     =     9     >>

Every slot starts blank; no gold token is ever in the input. Slot 8 must produce "9" without being given <<16-3-4= — it has to work it out from the question, through the loops, jointly with the other slots.

Why the last rows matter.

  • No argmax: the answer reads the hidden vectors, not decoded tokens, so a slot can hold "maybe 9, maybe 10" and still be useful. The paper's readouts look messy (e.g. "1818", repeated "18") yet the answer is right — the CoT only needs to be roughly there.
  • Small weight: in CoT, every reasoning token being exact is the whole job. In LOTUS the step loss only points the latents at the gold steps; the answer loss picks a version that actually works. Hence 70.9% gold top-1 with both losses, ~9% with either alone.
  • Not a chain: the step loss treats slots as independent (product of per-slot probabilities, not autoregressive). The dependence between steps is carried by the loops computing all slots jointly, not by the loss.

Where the extra compute goes

flowchart LR subgraph CoT["Explicit CoT: sideways"] direction LR c1[pass] --> c2[pass] --> c3[pass] --> c4["... N passes"] --> ca[answer] end subgraph LOTUS["LOTUS: upward, 150 slots in parallel"] direction LR l1[loop 1] --> l2[loop 2] --> l3["... loop 6"] --> la[answer] end

CoT pays one sequential pass per thinking token. LOTUS pays 6 sequential passes no matter how many slots, which is why widening c from 1 to 50 only moves thinking latency from 111 to 141 ms.

Things to keep in mind

  • K, c and R are fixed. Problems with more than 6 steps get no step supervision on the tail.
  • Only math (GSM8K-style) is tested, and the gold steps are short equations; the natural-language run needs c = 50.
  • It still depends on having gold CoT data — the latents are trained to imitate it, not discover new steps (though the readouts do put some mass on valid unseen paths: 64% of unseen-path numbers appear in the top-5).
