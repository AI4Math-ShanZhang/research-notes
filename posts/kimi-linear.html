Kimi Linear — An Expressive, Efficient Attention Architecture

Paper: Kimi Linear — Kimi Team (Moonshot AI), arXiv:2510.26692 · code. Technical report, v2 November 2025.

✅ Conclusion

  • The claim: first hybrid linear-attention architecture to beat full attention under fair comparison — short context, long context, and RL scaling — rather than merely trading quality for speed.
  • The core module: Kimi Delta Attention (KDA) extends Gated DeltaNet by replacing its single head-wise scalar forget gate with a per-channel gate, so every feature dimension forgets at its own rate.
  • The architecture: 3 KDA layers to 1 full MLA layer, and the MLA layers use NoPE — all positional information is delegated to KDA.
  • Numbers: 48B total / 3B activated, 1.4T tokens. Up to 75% less KV cache, 6× decoding throughput at 1M context, 1.16× compute efficiency on the scaling-law fit.
  • Not pure linear. One in four layers is still real softmax attention. The paper is explicit that finite-state memory can't do long-context retrieval alone.

📄 Paper Summary

The problem

Softmax attention has two costs that hurt most in agentic and RL settings, where models chew through long trajectories at inference time:

  • Quadratic time — every query is scored against every key.
  • A KV cache that grows linearly with context — you must keep every past key and value.

Linear attention removes both, but has historically been worse at language modelling, even on short sequences. Two lines of work narrowed the gap: gating/decay and the delta rule. This paper combines them, and then hedges the remainder with a few real softmax layers.

Why is it called "linear"? Two meanings

The starting equation in §2.2 is plain linear attention:

S_t = S_(t-1) + k_t v_t^T          state update
o_t = S_t^T q_t                    read out

There are two meanings of "linear" here, and the second follows from the first.

Meaning 1 — the output is a linear function of q

Softmax attention:

o_t = sum_i [ exp(q_t · k_i) / Z ] · v_i

The exp wraps the query–key dot product. Double q_t and the output does not double — softmax re-weights and sharpens instead.

Linear attention drops the exp:

o_t = S_t^T q_t

That is literally a matrix times a vector. Double q_t, and o_t doubles. That is the textbook definition of a linear map. Softmax attention is not one.

Meaning 2 — linear time, and here is why

Once the exp is gone, you can re-associate the matrix product. That is the whole trick:

o_t = sum_i (q_t · k_i) v_i
    = sum_i v_i (k_i^T q_t)          reorder — it's just a scalar
    = ( sum_i v_i k_i^T ) q_t        pull q_t OUT of the sum
    = S_t^T q_t                      where  S_t = sum_i k_i v_i^T

The critical line is the third: q_t escapes the sum. With exp(q_t · k_i) sitting there, it cannot — the query is trapped inside a nonlinear function, so you must evaluate it separately against every single key.

In matrix form, this is just associativity:

OrderWho must use itCost
(Q K^T) Vsoftmax — forced, because exp sits betweenT² · d
Q (K^T V)linear — allowedT · d²

With T = 1,000,000 and d = 128:

  • (QK^T)V ≈ 1.28 × 1014 operations
  • Q(K^T V) ≈ 1.6 × 1010 operations

About 8000× fewer. And K^T V is exactly S_t — a fixed d_k × d_v matrix that does not grow with context. That is why there is no KV cache, and why the paper reports 75% less KV memory and 6× decoding throughput at 1M context.

softmax:  keep all T (k,v) pairs  →  memory grows with context
linear:   keep one matrix S       →  memory is constant

Why does q_t need to escape the sum?

Because if q_t stays inside the sum, the sum is different for every query — so you have to redo it every time. If it escapes, the sum is the same for every query, so you compute it once and reuse it forever.

The real question: can you pre-combine k and v? 
q_t inside:   o_t = sum_i  f(q_t, k_i) · v_i     the summand depends on t AND i
q_t outside:  o_t = ( sum_i k_i v_i^T ) · q_t    the summand depends on i ONLY
Look at the second line. That sum has no q in it. It doesn't care which query is coming. So you can build it before any query arrives, and every future query reads the same object.

In the first line the weight on v_i depends on q_t, which hasn't arrived yet. So you're stuck keeping k_i and v_i apart, waiting.

Can you merge k_i and v_i early?Consequence
SoftmaxNo — the weight needs q, which hasn't arrivedmust store every k_i and v_i → KV cache
LinearYes — k_i v_i^T is self-containedstore only the running total → one matrix

A small worked example. Take d = 1 so everything is a plain number, four tokens:

k = [2, 3, 1, 4]
v = [10, 20, 30, 40]

Naive (q inside the sum) — the 4th query needs

o_4 = q_4·2·10 + q_4·3·20 + q_4·1·30 + q_4·4·40      4 terms

Query 3 needs 3 terms, query 2 needs 2, query 1 needs 1 — 10 terms total, i.e. T²/2.

Factored (q escapes) — build a running total instead:

S_1 = 20
S_2 = 20 + 60   = 80
S_3 = 80 + 30   = 110
S_4 = 110 + 160 = 270

Then every query is one multiply: o_4 = q_4 · 270. Total 4 adds + 4 multiplies, i.e. T. At T = 1,000,000 that's 1012 versus 106.

Why softmax can't do this. The weight exp(q_t · k_i) is a different number for every (t, i) pair, and exp refuses to factor over sums — exp(a+b) = exp(a)·exp(b) gives products, not sums. Attention needs sums to combine. The two don't fit.

Two things escaping buys you. (1) The sum is built once and shared by all T queries — compute drops from T² to T. (2) Once k_i v_i^T is folded into S, the individual k_i and v_i can be thrown away — which is why the update is written as a recurrence S_t = S_(t-1) + k_t v_t^T. Linear attention is an RNN with a matrix-valued state, and the escaping is what makes it one.

And the price. Reading out gives o_t = S_t^T q_t = sum_i (k_i^T q_t) v_i — you wanted v_3, you get v_3 plus a little of every other v_i, weighted by however much q_t happens to overlap k_i. Nothing suppresses the wrong ones, because there is no exp to sharpen them away. The paper says it directly: the objective "continually reinforces recent key–value pairs without any forgetting," so "the accumulated state grows unbounded, leading to interference over long contexts."

The ladder: fixing that interference without breaking linearity

Everything after §2.2 adds capability while carefully preserving the associativity that makes the whole thing linear. Each rung is an online-learning objective on the state S:

1. Linear attention — gradient descent on L_t(S) = −<S^T k_t, v_t> 
S_t = S_(t-1) + k_t v_t^T
Pure accumulation. No forgetting, no error correction.
2. DeltaNet — gradient descent on a reconstruction loss L_t(S) = ½‖S^T k_t − v_t‖² 
S_t = (I − β_t k_t k_t^T) S_(t-1) + β_t k_t v_t^T
The classical delta rule: the state corrects itself toward the mapping k_t → v_t. The rank-1 update is a generalized Householder transformation, which is what makes chunkwise parallel training possible.
3. Gated DeltaNet (GDN) — add a scalar forget gate α_t ∈ [0,1] 
S_t = α_t (I − β_t k_t k_t^T) S_(t-1) + β_t k_t v_t^T
α_t acts as weight decay on the fast weights — data-dependent L2 regularization, controlling memory lifespan.
4. KDA (this paper) — make the gate per-channel 
S_t = (I − β_t k_t k_t^T) Diag(α_t) S_(t-1) + β_t k_t v_t^T
Diag(α_t) instead of a scalar: every feature dimension gets its own forgetting rate. GDN and Mamba2 use one coarse head-wise gate; KDA borrows the channel-wise idea from GLA. With a finite-size state, how precisely you forget is how well you use the memory you have.

KDA parameterizes this as a specialized variant of Diagonal-Plus-Low-Rank (DPLR) transitions — cheaper than the general DPLR formulation while staying closer to the classical delta rule, which is what lets them write a bespoke chunkwise kernel.

Around the core, the neural parameterization is:

q_t, k_t = L2Norm(Swish(ShortConv(W x_t)))    L2Norm for eigenvalue stability
v_t      = Swish(ShortConv(W_v x_t))
α_t      = f(W↑ W↓ x_t) ∈ [0,1]^dk            per-channel decay, low-rank projection
β_t      = Sigmoid(W_β x_t)                    learning rate
o_t      = W_o [ Sigmoid(W↑_g W↓_g x_t) ⊙ RMSNorm(KDA(...)) ]

The output gate is low-rank and sigmoid — the paper notes it also alleviates the attention-sink problem.

The architecture

flowchart TD I[Input tokens] --> B1[KDA layer] B1 --> B2[KDA layer] B2 --> B3[KDA layer] B3 --> M[Full MLA layer · NoPE] M --> R[repeat ...] R --> O[Output]
  • 3:1 hybrid. Three KDA layers per one full MLA layer, chosen layerwise (whole layers alternate) rather than headwise, for infrastructure simplicity and training stability.
  • NoPE on the MLA layers. No positional encoding at all on the full-attention layers — KDA carries all positional information. This also lets MLA convert to efficient MQA at inference and removes RoPE frequency-base tuning from long-context training.
  • MoE backbone following Moonlight: 8 of 256 experts activated plus one shared expert, first layer dense for stability. 48B total, 3B activated.

Why NoPE works here (§6.1)

Write attention in a generalized form where the query–key score carries a cumulative product of transition matrices between positions i and t. RoPE makes that transition a fixed 2D rotation with hand-set frequencies. The gated delta rule makes it A_j (I − β_j k_j k_j^T) — data-dependent and learned, and free of RoPE's orthogonality constraint.

So KDA is a positional encoding, just a learnable one. That is the argument for giving MLA NoPE: if the linear layers already encode position, adding RoPE on top puts a strong explicit short-range bias in the global layer, which helps short contexts but hurts adaptation to long ones. The numbers agree — Kimi Linear (RoPE) matches on short context but drops to 51.8 long-context average against 54.5.

The honest framing

Kimi Linear is not a pure linear-attention model. One layer in four is full softmax MLA, and the paper states plainly why: "purely linear structure remain fundamentally constrained by the finite-state capacity, making long-sequence modeling and in-context retrieval theoretically challenging." The 3:1 ablation confirms it — push to 15:1 and validation PPL degrades to 5.82, worse than pure full attention's 5.77. The contribution is finding the ratio where the crosstalk stays survivable, plus a gate fine-grained enough to make each linear layer worth its slot.
