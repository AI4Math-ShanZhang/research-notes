DreamWAM — Motion, Depth and DINO as Training-Only Future Targets

Paper: DreamWAM: Beyond RGB Future Prediction for World Action Models — Yuan, Zhao, Shi, Jiang, Guo, Liu, Liu, Sui & Wang (HUST · D-Robotics · Wuhan U · Horizon Robotics), Aug 2026, arXiv:2608.04996 · code. This note walks through the model step by step with tensor shapes, then the questions I had to work out: why flow can be denoised like RGB but depth and DINO cannot, what each teacher outputs, the losses, and the train/test gap.

📄 Paper Summary

The idea in one line

train:  VideoDiT predicts future  RGB + optical flow (denoised)  + depth + DINO (gated residual heads)
        ActionDiT shares attention with VideoDiT → learns from this richer future
test:   plain Fast-WAM — RGB only, flow channels = 0, all extra branches and teachers removed

Step by step

  1. Start from Fast-WAM. Two experts with their own weights. Paired layers put video and action tokens into one attention, so the action expert can read what the video expert is modelling.
  2. Make three extra future targets offline from every 9-frame training clip (all teachers frozen): 
    RGB clip                      9 × 3 × H × W        (H×W ≈ 224×448, two views side by side — my inference)

motion:  RAFT on 8 pairs  →   8 × 2 × H × W        (dx, dy per pixel)
         colour-code      →   8 × 3 × H × W
         copy first       →   9 × 3 × H × W
         Wan2.2 VAE       →  48 × 3 × 14 × 28      ← same as RGB latent

depth:   DA3-Base per view → depth map per frame
         log, 14×28 cells, PCA-8, time 9→3   →   8 × 3 × 14 × 28

DINO:    DINOv2 ViT-B/14 (registers), 768-d patches
         PCA-8, resize 14×28, time 9→3, norm →   8 × 3 × 14 × 28

time 9→3 = keep frame 1 | mean of frames 2–5 | mean of frames 6–9
  3. RGB + motion: joint denoising. Same flow-matching time t for both: x = (1−t)·z + t·noise. Stack them on channels → 96 × 3 × 14 × 28 into the VideoDiT.
  4. Depth + DINO: gated residual branches. In chosen VideoDiT layers: 
    h̄  = Block(h_prev)
h  = h̄ + g_geo(h̄, c) ⊙ R_geo(h̄)  +  g_sem(h̄, c) ⊙ R_sem(h̄)
f̂_j = P_j(h_last)        → compared with the 8 × 3 × 14 × 28 target
    The main stream h̄ stays intact; the branches only add a small, gated correction.
  5. Loss. 
    L = λ_rgb·L_FM(rgb) + λ_mot·L_FM(mot)          ← flow matching on latents
  + λ_geo·L_pred(geo) + λ_sem·L_pred(sem)        ← feature prediction from P_j
  + λ_act·L_act                                  ← action flow matching
  + λ_g·L_gate                                   ← L1 on gate values (keep gates small)
    The λ values and the exact form of L_pred (MSE / cosine) are not given.
  6. Inference. Same interface as Fast-WAM. Flow channels are filled with zeros, so the first layer becomes W_rgb·z_rgb + b. RAFT, DA3, DINOv2, the residual branches and the heads are all removed. Output: the denoised action chunk only.

🖊 My Key Notes

At test time this is exactly Fast-WAM. Motion, depth and DINO only shape the shared VideoDiT weights during training. Same bet as InternW0-Δ: use the future as a training signal, then switch it off.

Q&A — why flow goes with RGB, but depth and DINO do not

What is the motion input, and why can it be processed the same as RGB?

Motion is RAFT optical flow between neighbouring frames, drawn as a colour picture (colour = direction, brightness = speed). A colour picture is just a normal 3-channel image, so it can go through the same Wan2.2 VAE and comes out as 48 × 3 × 14 × 28 — exactly the RGB latent shape. Same shape → stack on channels → denoise both with one VideoDiT at the same noise level.

Does that mean the Wan VAE was trained on flow?

No. The Wan VAE was trained on normal videos/images. It does not need to know the picture is flow — it is a general image compressor, and flow pictures are smooth colour patches, easy to compress. The correct claim is flow can be put into the same format as RGB, not that the VAE knows flow. Two things the paper does not check: how well the VAE reconstructs flow pictures (blur = lost fine motion), and the VideoDiT never saw flow latents in pretraining either — it learns them during DreamWAM fine-tuning.

Why can't depth and DINO be denoised the same way?

They are not pictures, so they can't go through the VAE. They come from other networks with different channel counts and value ranges. Stacking them as extra denoising channels forces the whole VideoDiT to model foreign statistics, which the authors say damages the pretrained video prior. The ablation backs this: geometry by denoising 96.80, by residual 98.15; semantics 96.80 vs 98.10; all-denoise 97.85, below the RGB-only 98.00. So technically you can denoise them — it just works worse.

Q&A — the teachers

What does RAFT take in and give out?
input:   frame t     3 × H × W
         frame t+1   3 × H × W
output:  flow        2 × H × W      (dx, dy per pixel, full resolution)

example pixel: dx = 3, dy = −1  →  moves 3 px right, 1 px up

Per 9-frame clip: 8 × 2 × H × W raw flow → 8 × 3 × H × W colour pictures → copy first → 9 × 3 × H × W → VAE → 48 × 3 × 14 × 28. The paper does not give H × W (≈224 × 448 inferred from the 14 × 28 latent and the VAE's 16× spatial shrink), and does not say whether RAFT runs per camera view or on the joined image — on the joined image, flow near the seam would be wrong.

Is RAFT a neural network or a classical method?

A deep neural network (Teed & Deng, ECCV 2020), about 5M parameters, trained supervised on flow datasets (FlyingChairs, FlyingThings, Sintel, KITTI). Three parts: a CNN encoder (features at 1/8 size), an all-pairs correlation volume (every pixel of frame 1 vs every pixel of frame 2), and a GRU that starts from flow = 0 and refines it 12–32 times, then upsamples. The classical, non-learned methods are Lucas–Kanade and Horn–Schunck. In DreamWAM RAFT is frozen and used offline only.

What does Depth Anything V3 output, and what size is the target?

DA3-Base outputs one depth map per camera view (one depth value per pixel). Raw resolution is not stated. Build steps: split the two views → DA3 on each → join back → log depth → cut into a 14 × 28 grid, each cell one local descriptor → PCA to 8 channels (fit on training data only) → time 9 → 3. Final: 8 × 3 × 14 × 28. DINO follows the same recipe from 768-d patch features, also 8 × 3 × 14 × 28.

Shape check (my own): Wan2.2 VAE shrinks 16× in space and 4× in time → 224 × 448 / 16 = 14 × 28, and 9 frames → 1 + 8/4 = 3 time steps. Consistent.

Q&A — losses

What are the geometry, semantic and gate losses?
  • Geometry / semantic: head P_j (a DPT head) reads the last VideoDiT hidden state and predicts the 8 × 3 × 14 × 28 target; the loss compares it with the precomputed DA3 / DINO target. Form not given.
  • Gate: L1 penalty on gate values → gates are pushed toward 0, so the branches can only make small corrections and never replace the main video signal.
  • None of the λ weights are reported.

Q&A — the train / test gap

The motion input is off and the branches are removed at inference. Does that gap hurt?

Yes, there is a real gap; the net effect is still positive; its cost is never measured.

Gap A — zero flow channels. At inference the 48 flow channels are zeros, so the input layer becomes W_rgb·z_rgb + b. Training only partly matches: the flow channels of the current frame are zero in training too, but the future flow channels are always noisy flow latents, never zeros. In joint mode the future tokens see an input they never saw in training.

Gap B — removed residual branches. Training: h = h̄ + g·R. Inference: h = h̄. The ActionDiT learned to attend to the version with residuals. The L1 gate penalty is what makes this safe:

|h̄| ≈ 1.0,  |g·R| ≈ 0.02   →  removing the branch shifts the state by ≈ 2%

But actual gate values are not reported.

What the evidence says: every reported number is measured with the gap, so the gains (e.g. 51.36 → 63.44) are what is left after paying for it. Two easy ablations are missing: (1) keep the residual branches on at inference — they only need h̄, not DA3 or DINO, so this is free; (2) start the flow channels from noise and denoise them with RGB instead of filling with zeros.

Interesting side number: the gap between no-rollout and joint inference shrinks from 17.80 (Fast-WAM) to 12.02 (DreamWAM) on LIBERO-Plus. Part of what test-time imagination supplied is now baked into the weights — training-time future structure and test-time rollout are complementary, not interchangeable.
