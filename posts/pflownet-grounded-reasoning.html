PFlowNet — Don't Copy the Detector's Box, Stay Near It

Paper: Perceptual Flow Network for Visually Grounded Reasoning — Li, Gong, Zhan, Li, Lyu, Chen, Liu, Huang, Zhong, Zheng & Lu (ECNU · SCU · HKUST · SJTU · Ant Group · Shanghai AI Lab), arXiv:2605.02730. ICML 2026, May 2026.

✅ Conclusion

  • The finding that starts everything: they grew the detector's box outward from its center and fed only that crop to the model. The tightest, most precise box gave worse answers than a looser one. A tight crop cuts away context the model needs — they call it tunnel vision. So training a VLM to match GroundingDINO exactly is aiming at the wrong target.
  • Why you can't just fix it by hand: the right amount of looseness is different for every image and every question. No fixed rule works.
  • The move: replace the hard "match the expert box" constraint with a learned distribution over perceptual flows — plan → (box + caption) × K → answer. The geometry term only says stay inside a ball of radius ε around the expert box; inside that ball the model is free.
  • How the step-wise signal appears: the reward is defined on partial flows (after 1 box, after 2 boxes, …), so every prefix has its own scalar. SubTB's flow-conservation constraint between every pair of prefixes turns those scalars into a per-step gradient — no value network.
  • Result: on Qwen3-VL-8B — V* 90.6 (+13), MME-RealWorld-lite 67.0 (+18.4), TreeBench 55.3 (+10.4). The 8B model beats the 4× bigger Qwen3-VL-32B on the hard visual-search tasks.
  • The theory framing: λ→0 degenerates to plain MLE, λ→∞ degenerates to expert-forced RLVR. Both existing approaches are special cases; the in-between is provably tighter.

📄 Paper Summary

The problem they found

Vision-language models hallucinate. The popular fix: run a detector like GroundingDINO, get a box around the important object, then train the model with RL so its own box matches that box. The detector box is the expert prior.

They tested whether this is actually good. On V* Bench, they took the expert box and slowly grew it outward from the center, then fed only that crop to the model and measured accuracy.

Counter-intuitive result. The tightest box was not the best. Accuracy peaked at some medium looseness. A tight crop removes the surrounding context the model needs to reason. The most geometrically precise evidence is not the most useful evidence.

Follow-up problem: the best looseness is instance-specific. You cannot write it down as a heuristic.

Perceptual Flow

The model's visual thinking, written out as a chain:

  • Planning state z0 (inside <analyze> tags): plain text that breaks the question down and lists what to look for.
  • Perceptual states z1 … zK (inside <localize> tags): each one is a box (relative coordinates 0–1000) plus a caption describing what is in it.

Decoupled, two stages

First generate the whole flow. Then crop those boxes out of the image, feed the crops back in, and only then write the answer. The answer is conditioned on the model's own perception instead of being tangled together with it.

Training, three parts

  1. Data. Take samples that have detector boxes. Randomly expand each box first — on purpose, to kill the detector bias. Teacher models (Gemini 3 Flash, GPT-4o) write the plan and the per-crop captions. A verifier then answers each sample twice, with the flow and without it. Trivial samples and useless flows get dropped; the rest split into a cold-start SFT set and an RL set.
  2. Cold start. Ordinary supervised fine-tuning on those synthetic flows.
  3. Variational RFT. Sub-Trajectory Balance (from GFlowNets) instead of PPO, because it gives a signal at every step of the chain rather than only at the end.

The three reward pieces

PieceWhat it measures
Quality For each box: how likely is this caption given the crop inside the box, versus given the image with that region cut out. A high ratio means the caption really describes that region and is not generic filler.
Efficacy Does having this flow raise the probability of the correct final answer? The "was this perception actually useful" term.
Geometry
(vicinal shaping)		 Instead of forcing a match, draw a ball of radius ε around the detector box and penalize only for leaving it. Inside the ball, the model picks whatever box helps reasoning most.

The theory part

They bound the distance between what the model learns and the ideal target. Two limits fall out:

  • λ → 0: the geometry term vanishes → plain supervised training (MLE).
  • λ → ∞: → normal expert-forced RL, stuck at the detector's bias.

With λ and ε tuned in between, the bound is strictly tighter than both. Both existing approaches are special cases of theirs, so theirs is provably at least as good.

🖊 My Key Notes

Q1 — How does the paper use SubTB, and where does the step-wise supervision come from?

First: what is a "step" here?

Not a token. One step = one perceptual state z_k = (one box + its caption). A flow with 3 RoIs has 4 states: z0 (the plan), z1, z2, z3.

The probability of one step, p_theta(z_k | z_0:k-1), is the product of the token probabilities of that whole block — the box coordinates plus the caption text.

⊤ = the terminal state = emitting the </localize> token, i.e. "I stop looking, now answer".

The key move: make the reward computable at every prefix

R(z_0:k , ⊤)  =  [ prod_{i=1..k}  p+(z_i) / p-(z_i) ]  *  p(Y | z_0:k , ⊤ , X)

The product runs i = 1..k — it is a running product. And the second term asks: "if I stop right now, after only k boxes, how likely is the correct answer Y?"

This is the whole trick. You can evaluate the reward after 1 box, after 2 boxes, after 3 boxes. Every prefix gets its own scalar. The step-wise signal does not come from a value network — it comes from the reward being defined on partial flows.

Then the geometry gate multiplies in:

R_lambda(z_0:k, ⊤) = R(z_0:k, ⊤) * omega_lambda(z_0:k, E)

omega = exp(-lambda)  if the boxes so far wandered outside the eps-ball of the expert prior
      = 1             if inside

How SubTB uses it

A GFlowNet has a state flow F(z). The paper does not learn F — it defines it from the reward:

F(z_i) = R_lambda(z_0:i , ⊤) / p_theta(⊤ | z_0:i)

  "total flow through state i"  =  "reward if you stop here" / "probability you stop here"

SubTB then requires, for every pair of prefixes i < j, that flow is conserved:

F(z_i) * p_theta(z_i+1 | ...) * ... * p_theta(z_j | ...)  =  F(z_j)

Substituting F gives the paper's Eq. 2. In log space the residual for pair (i, j) is:

res(i,j) = log R_i - log R_j
         + sum_{k=i+1..j} log p_theta(z_k | z_0:k-1)
         + log p_theta(⊤ | z_0:j)
         - log p_theta(⊤ | z_0:i)

Loss = sum of res(i,j)² over all pairs. With K states that is about K²/2 constraints per sample — that is the "dense supervision". (Appendix B.4 notes many pairs share prefixes, so the shared computation is batched.)

Small notation catch. Eq. 1 has a backward term T_B, but Eq. 2 does not. That is because a perceptual flow is a straight chain, not a branching graph — going backward is deterministic, probability 1, so it drops out.

Now check two pairs. Say the current policy has p_theta(z2|z0:1) = 0.05, p_theta(z3|z0:2) = 0.40, and stop-probabilities p(⊤|z0:1) = 0.3, p(⊤|z0:2) = 0.3, p(⊤|z0:3) = 0.5.

Pair (1, 2):

res = log(0.40) - log(3.60) + log(0.05) + log(0.30) - log(0.30)
    = -0.916 - 1.281 + (-3.00) + (-1.204) - (-1.204)
    = -5.20        (squared: 27.0)

Negative and big → left side too small → the gradient pushes p_theta(z2 | z0:1) up, and pushes the stop-probability after box 1 down. Meaning: "adding box 2 tripled the reward, so take that step more often, and do not stop at box 1."

Pair (2, 3):

res = log(3.60) - log(0.607) + log(0.40) + log(0.50) - log(0.30)
    = 1.281 + 0.499 - 0.916 - 0.693 + 1.204
    = +1.375       (squared: 1.89)

Positive → left side too big → push p_theta(z3 | z0:2) down, and push the stop-probability after box 2 up. Meaning: "box 3 hurt, stop after two."

The point. Blame lands exactly on the 2→3 transition. Boxes 1 and 2 are untouched.

Why not PPO

With PPO you get one number at the end — here that would be 0.607 for the whole trajectory. A critic network has to guess how to split that across the three steps, and the guess is bootstrapped and noisy. Worse, PPO's objective pushes mass onto the single best trajectory, so the policy gets sharp — which is exactly why the paper's Figure 7 shows TreeVGR's Pass@k flattening out.

SubTB targets come straight from the reward at each prefix, no critic. And it is a distribution-matching objective — it tries to make p(Z) proportional to R(Z), so many good flows keep nonzero mass. That is why their Pass@k keeps climbing to k=8.

Q2 — Why is the caption ratio p+/p- a scalar, and not something of size |V|?

p+ is not a next-token distribution. It is the likelihood of one specific caption string that was already sampled.

p+(z_i) = p_phi( c_i | I_ri )

c_i is a fixed string — the caption the policy already wrote for box i, e.g. "a white spherical sculpture on a wooden side table". I_ri is the crop.

To get that number you run the frozen reward model on the crop, teacher-force the caption, and multiply the token probabilities:

p+(z_i) = prod_{t=1..|c_i|}  p_phi( c_i[t] | c_i[<t], I_ri )
Where |V| goes. Each factor is one entry picked out of a |V| softmax — the entry for the token that actually appears in c_i. You throw away the other |V|−1 entries. Multiply across positions → one scalar.

p- is the same string scored against the image with that region cut out.

Caption = "a red car", 3 tokens.

One number. Read it as: "this caption is 5× more likely when you can see inside the box than when you cannot." So the caption really describes that region. If the caption were generic filler like "an object in a room", both columns would be nearly equal, the ratio would sit near 1.0, and there would be no reward.

Why it has to be a scalar

GFlowNet flow conservation is scalar arithmetic:

F(z_i) * p_theta(z_i+1 | z_i) = F(z_j)

F is a non-negative real number — "how much probability mass flows through this state". You cannot conserve a |V|-dimensional flow; there is nothing to multiply and balance. So the reward feeding into F must collapse to a single non-negative number.

Also: p_phi is frozen. The ratio is a constant with respect to θ, computed once per sampled flow. No gradient passes through it — it behaves like a reward handed back by an environment.

The |V| dimension does appear in training, but on the other side: inside log p_theta(z_k | z_0:k-1) in the SubTB residual. That is where backprop touches the logits.

The one place a full distribution shows up

In the "Key Insights in Reward Design" paragraph the paper takes the expectation of that log-ratio over sampled captions:

E_{c ~ q_theta} [ log p+/p- ]
    =  KL( q_theta || p_phi(·|image minus crop) )  -  KL( q_theta || p_phi(·|crop) )

Now p_phi(·|crop) with the dot is a full distribution — but over the space of all caption strings, not over |V| tokens. And this is only the analysis: it explains why maximizing the ratio pulls the policy's captions toward the "privileged" teacher that saw the crop and away from the one that did not. Per sample, you still only compute the scalar ratio.
